> [!note] Fictional example. Saltmere, its storm of 14 and 15 March 2026 and every account quoted here are invented, to show what a note can hold. The photographs are real public-domain pictures of other storms and places, used as illustrations: each caption says where and when it was really taken.

[[media:{{quay}}|Yachts thrown onto the marina quay]]{width=100% caption="The marina quay on the morning of 15 March. Illustration: FEMA photo by Walter Jennings, Bayland Marina, Baytown, Texas, 21 September 2008, public domain; the scene and dates of this example are invented."}

The storm reached Saltmere a little after ten at night on 14 March. By first light the marina was a yard of hulls on their sides, Beach Road lay under sand, and a post going round said every house at the west end had been flattened. **At least 16 boats were lost or damaged, 2 of them sunk. The houses still stand.**

Every figure here is a minimum: it counts only what the pictures show.

## The marina at first light

[[media:{{marina}}|The marina canal on 15 March]]{width=100% caption="The marina canal on 15 March. Illustration: FEMA photo by Liz Roll, Pleasure Island marina, Port Arthur, Texas, 20 September 2008, public domain; the scene and dates of this example are invented."}

The surge lifted boats off their moorings and set them down on the bank, some still on their cradles. One yacht stayed afloat in the canal, its deck full of the reeds the water left behind.

[[media:{{salt-marsh}}|A yacht carried into the salt marsh]]{width=100% caption="A yacht carried into the salt marsh, seen from the drone flight of 16 March. Illustration: U.S. Coast Guard photo by Jessica Potter, near Atlantic Beach, North Carolina, 28 August 2011, public domain; the scene and dates of this example are invented."}

The water carried one yacht across the road and into the salt marsh, where the drone flight of 16 March found it upright in the grass.

## Beach Road, before and after

[[media:{{pair}}|Beach Road before and after the storm]]{width=100% caption="Beach Road before and after the storm. Illustration: NOAA aerial photographs of St. George Island, Florida, April 2017 and 13 October 2018, public domain; the places and dates of this example are invented."}

From the air, before and after: the sea came over the dunes, flattened them and left its sand across the road, its whole length.

## What fell, and what did not

[[media:{{proof-houses}}|The houses at the west end stand]]{width=75% caption="A house at the west end on 15 March. Illustration: FEMA photo by Mark Wolfe, Pensacola Beach, Florida, 15 October 2004, public domain; the scene and dates of this example are invented."}

The post that said every house at the west end had been flattened ([[entity:{{post-915}}|@saltmere_breaking, 15 Mar]]) came with no picture. The same morning the houses stood behind the sand the sea left, roofs torn and patched, walls whole ([[entity:{{proof-houses}}|proof]]). What the sea did take was the end of the old pier ([[entity:{{proof-pier}}|proof]]).

> [!limits] The pictures cover the marina, Beach Road and the beach. Nothing here says what the storm did inland, or to anyone.
