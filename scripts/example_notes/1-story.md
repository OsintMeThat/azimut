> [!note] Fictional example. Saltmere, its storm of 14 and 15 March 2026 and every account quoted here are invented, to show what a note can hold. The photographs are real, public-domain pictures of other storms and places, each credited under it.

[[media:{{quay}}|Yachts thrown onto the marina quay]]{width=100% caption="The marina quay on the morning of 15 March. Photo: Walter Jennings, FEMA (Bayland Marina, Texas, 2008), public domain."}

The storm reached Saltmere a little after ten at night on 14 March. By first light the marina was a yard of hulls on their sides, Beach Road lay under sand, and a post going round said the lighthouse had fallen. **At least 16 boats were lost or damaged, 2 of them sunk. The lighthouse still stands.**

Every figure here is a minimum: it counts only what the pictures show.

## The marina at first light

[[media:{{marina}}|The marina canal on 15 March]]{width=100% caption="The marina canal on 15 March. Photo: Liz Roll, FEMA (Port Arthur, Texas, 2008), public domain."}

The surge lifted boats off their moorings and set them down on the bank, some still on their cradles. One yacht stayed afloat in the canal, its deck full of the reeds the water left behind.

[[media:{{salt-marsh}}|A yacht carried into the salt marsh]]{width=100% caption="A yacht carried into the salt marsh east of the marina, seen from the drone flight of 16 March. Photo: Jessica Potter, U.S. Coast Guard (North Carolina, 2011), public domain."}

The water carried one yacht across the road and into the salt marsh, where the drone flight of 16 March found it upright in the grass.

## Beach Road, before and after

[[media:{{pair}}|Beach Road before and after the storm]]{width=100% caption="Beach Road on 2 March and on 16 March. NOAA aerial photographs (Mexico Beach, Florida, 2017 and 2018), public domain."}

From the air, two weeks apart: the road is gone under sand, and most of the houses between it and the beach have lost their roofs or their walls.

## What fell, and what did not

[[media:{{proof-lighthouse}}|Cape Hallow lighthouse stands]]{width=50% caption="Cape Hallow lighthouse on 16 March. Photo: David A. Weydert, U.S. Coast Guard (Bodie Island, North Carolina, 2011), public domain."}

The post that said the lighthouse had fallen ([[entity:{{post-915}}|@saltmere_breaking, 15 Mar]]) came with no picture. The next morning the lighthouse stood whole, water round its foot ([[entity:{{proof-lighthouse}}|proof]]). What fell was the light at the end of the old pier, whose deck the sea took ([[entity:{{proof-pier}}|proof]]).

> [!limits] The pictures cover the marina, Beach Road and the beach. Nothing here says what the storm did inland, or to anyone.
