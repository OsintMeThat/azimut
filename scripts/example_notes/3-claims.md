## Claim 1: the lighthouse

> "The lighthouse at Cape Hallow has fallen." ([[entity:{{post-915}}|@saltmere_breaking, 15 Mar]])

The post came with no picture. Pictures of the old pier taken the same morning show its deck gone beyond the surf line, and with it the small light at its end ([[entity:{{proof-pier}}|proof]]). The lighthouse at Cape Hallow is another structure, 2 km to the south-east, and on 16 March it stood whole ([[entity:{{proof-lighthouse}}|proof]]).

[[media:{{proof-pier}}|The old pier lost its end]]{width=75% caption="The old pier on 15 March. Photo: Marvin Nauman, FEMA (Grand Isle, Louisiana, 2005), public domain."}

> [!warning] False. What fell was the light on the old pier, not the lighthouse.

## Claim 2: the launch

> "The island launch sank in the harbour." ([[entity:{{post-917}}|@saltmere_breaking, 15 Mar]])

[[media:{{proof-launch}}|The launch is ashore, not sunk]]{width=75% caption="The launch on the beach on 15 March. Photo: Jocelyn Augustino, FEMA (Pensacola Beach, Florida, 2005), public domain."}

> [!warning] False. The launch lies upright on the beach west of the old pier, above the tide line.

## Claim 3: fifty boats

> "Fifty boats destroyed at Saltmere." ([[entity:{{post-921}}|@saltmere_breaking, 16 Mar]])

The register counts at least 16 boats lost or damaged, 2 of them sunk ([[entity:{{note-2-register}}|the register]]). Fifty is more than the marina held.

> [!unverified] Misleading. At least 16 boats, of which 11 were carried ashore and 2 sank.

## Corrections

17 March 2026: the first edition counted at least 15 boats. The drone flight of 16 March found a sixteenth in the salt marsh, and the count now reads at least 16.
